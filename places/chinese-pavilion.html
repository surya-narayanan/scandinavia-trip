The Chinese Pavilion

Kina slott

A rococo-chinoiserie palace given as a surprise birthday present in 1753, at the end of the gardens.

Day 16 · Sun Aug 23 · 14:15–15:15A ten-minute garden walk from the theatre, and the first thing to cut if the group is tiring — the gardens alone are enough, and they are free.
First version1753, prefabricated in secret as a birthday gift
Present building1763–1769
StyleEuropean rococo interpretation of Chinese design
Cost~SEK 120 at the door; gardens free

In 1753 King Adolf Frederick had a Chinese-style pavilion built in secret, prefabricated in sections, and assembled in the gardens as a surprise for Queen Lovisa Ulrika’s thirty-third birthday. She was reportedly delighted. That first building deteriorated and was replaced by the present one, built between 1763 and 1769.

It is chinoiserie rather than Chinese: a European rococo fantasy of what Chinese architecture and decoration might be, done at a moment when Sweden’s East India Company was bringing real lacquer, porcelain and silk into Gothenburg. Some of the objects inside are genuinely Chinese imports; the framework around them is entirely Swedish imagination, and the tension between the two is what makes it interesting.

The interiors are small, intensely decorated rooms in green, blue and gold. Around it are outbuildings including the Confidencen dining pavilion, where a table could be raised through the floor already laid, so the royal party could eat without servants present.
