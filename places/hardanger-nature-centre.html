Norwegian Nature Centre Hardanger

Hardangervidda Natursenter

Ninety minutes indoors on how the fjords and the plateau were made — and the day’s insurance against rain.

Not currently in the planNot in the plan any more. Aug 19 was rebuilt on Aug 17 as a public-transport day to Norheimsund — one bus each way for the shipyard and Steinsdalsfossen — instead of the NOK 2,600 coach tour that reached this stop. It is left here because it is a genuinely good stop and because the tour has free cancellation up to 24 hours, so the decision is reversible.
WhereØvre Eidfjord, at the mouth of Måbødalen
CoversGlaciation, fjord formation, Hardangervidda ecology, the reindeer herds
Time1½ hrs — the longest single stop of the day
IndoorsEntirely

The centre sits at Øvre Eidfjord where the Måbødalen valley opens out, and it exists to explain the landscape you spend the rest of the day driving through. The core of it is glaciation: how ice sheets a kilometre thick gouged the fjords below sea level, how the Hardangervidda plateau was scraped flat above them, and what happened when the ice left.

There is a large-format film shown on a wraparound screen, geological and ecological displays, and material on the Hardangervidda’s wild reindeer — Europe’s largest remaining herd — and on the Norwegian Wild Reindeer Centre’s work. There is an aquarium of local freshwater fish and a café.

It is a good museum and it is also, practically, ninety minutes of warmth and dry in the middle of a day built around standing next to waterfalls.
