Atlanterhavsparken

The Atlantic Sea Park

One of the largest saltwater aquariums in northern Europe, built into the rock on the open Atlantic coast.

Day 9 · Sun Aug 16 · 15:00–17:00Three kilometres out, ~NOK 180 by taxi, and you should book the return when you arrive because Sunday evening taxis thin out. Check feeding times the moment you are through the door — that is what the visit is built around. The outdoor seal enclosure means shells.
Opened1998
LocationTueneset, 3 km west of the centre, on the open sea
Signature tank4 million litres, fed directly from the Atlantic
Cost~NOK 265
FeedingsDivers feed the big tank daily — check times on arrival

Rather than trucking in tropical species, Atlanterhavsparken does the local sea: it is cut into the coastal rock at Tueneset with the Atlantic breaking outside, and its tanks are fed straight from it. That gives cold, clean, moving water and lets it keep the species that actually live here — cod, halibut, wolffish, lumpsuckers, catsharks, and the kelp forests they live in.

The centrepiece is a four-million-litre tank with a viewing window several metres across, fed by the open sea, where divers hand-feed the fish daily while talking to the audience through a mask microphone. Outside there are pools for grey seals and penguins, and a coastal path around the headland.

It is a genuinely good aquarium and a slightly odd one, because the best exhibit is arguably the window itself: the tank is built so that the rock and kelp inside continue visually into the sea beyond.
