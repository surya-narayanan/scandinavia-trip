Akvariet i Bergen

The Bergen Aquarium

Norway’s largest aquarium, on the tip of the Nordnes peninsula — and, by most local accounts, primarily a place to take children.

Not currently in the planIn the plan now — Aug 19, 10:00–11:50. Two things put it there. Skipping Atlanterhavsparken removed the duplication argument, and dropping the Hardanger trip created a second Bergen day with room on the Nordnes side of the harbour. It opens at 10:00 and the sea bath is a few minutes further out on the same peninsula, so the two make one trip. The Bergen Card gives 25% off from March to October, not free entry — it is free only in winter — so this is one of the few things on these two days you still pay real money for.
WhereNordnesbakken 4, ~20 min walk west of the Fish Market
Open10:00–18:00 in summer
Size60-plus tanks; penguins, sea lions, seals, otters, tropical hall
Time needed1½–2 hrs

The aquarium opened in 1960 on the outer tip of Nordnes, where the water is clean and the peninsula runs out into the fjord. It holds more than sixty tanks, a large outdoor complex for penguins, sea lions and seals, a tropical section with snakes and crocodiles, and a walk-through kelp and North Sea display.

It is well run and well liked, and the consistent local framing of it is as a family destination — the recommendation people make is “if you are visiting with kids.” For two adults and two parents with a single day in a UNESCO harbour city, that is the honest assessment of where it sits.

Nordnes is a pleasant walk in its own right: quiet painted timber streets, and Nordnes Sjøbad, a heated outdoor seawater pool with a sauna, right beside the aquarium.
