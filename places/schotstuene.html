Schøtstuene and the Hanseatic collection

The assembly halls where the Hanseatic merchants ate, drank, held court and were forbidden to light a fire at home.

Do not walk to Finnegården on Bryggen expecting to get in: that building has been closed for restoration since 2018 and stays shut until about 2027.

WhereØvregaten 50

No fires were permitted in the timber warehouses of Bryggen — the fire risk was existential and the rule was absolute. So the German merchants built separate communal buildings up the hill where cooking and heating were allowed: the Schøtstuene, or assembly rooms. Each trading house had one, and this is where its men gathered to eat hot food, drink, settle disputes, hold their own courts and administer the brutal initiation rites imposed on apprentices.

They are panelled wooden rooms with long tables, box beds, painted decoration and enormous fireplaces, and they explain the daily texture of the Hanseatic period better than the wharf itself does — because this is where the people actually lived when they were not working.

With Finnegården closed, the Hanseatic Museum’s exhibitions have moved here, so this is currently the only place to see the collection at all. There is also material on the restoration in progress.
