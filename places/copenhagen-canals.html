The Copenhagen canals

An hour on the water that strings together most of the city’s waterfront set pieces in one pass.

Copenhagen is built on and around water, and the low open boats are the only way to see the parts of it that face the canals rather than the streets. The standard hour-long circuit leaves the inner canal at Gammel Strand, runs out into the main harbour past the Black Diamond library and the Opera House, loops up to the Little Mermaid, then comes back through the Christianshavn canals.

Christianshavn is the stretch worth staying awake for. Christian IV laid it out in 1618 on Dutch principles — a grid of islands separated by canals, with ramparts around the outside — to house merchants and a naval yard. The canal banks are lined with 18th-century warehouses, houseboats and small wooden craft, and the boats pass under bridges low enough that the guide will tell you to sit down.

What you see from the water that you cannot from land

The corkscrew spire of Our Saviour’s from below; the Royal Danish Playhouse and the Opera House facing each other across the harbour; the Circle Bridge; and the working harbour edge of a capital that has turned its industrial waterfront into swimming baths within one generation. In August the harbour baths are full of people, which tells you more about Copenhagen than most museums will.
