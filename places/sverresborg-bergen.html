Sverresborg

A grass hill above the fortress with extremely steep, unfenced sides — and the oldest fortified ground in Bergen.

Day 11 · Tue Aug 18 · 16:15–16:45Thirty minutes, free, and immediately above the fortress you are already in — look for the cannons on the hillside and you cannot miss the way up. Mind the edges: the sides are very steep and there are no railings. Soldiers walking about is normal; the area is still military. This is the first thing to cut if the tower ran long.

King Sverre built a fort on this rock in the 1180s during the Norwegian civil wars, and it was stormed and destroyed in 1198 by the Bagler faction, who reportedly threw the defenders off the cliffs. What survives is earthwork and rubble under grass, plus later gun positions — there is no building to visit.

What there is, is height and quiet. The hill stands directly over Bergenhus and the harbour mouth, which is obviously why it was fortified, and from the top you get the fortress, Bryggen and the fjord entrance in one view with almost nobody else up there.

Two cautions, both real. The sides are steep, unfenced and grassed, which is a bad combination in wet weather. And the surrounding area remains in military use, so keep to the open ground and expect to see personnel.
