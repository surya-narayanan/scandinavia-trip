Frederik’s Church

Frederiks Kirke — the Marmorkirken

Scandinavia’s largest church dome, on an axis with the royal palace, and a century and a half late.

Day 5 · Wed Aug 12 · 13:15–13:30Fifteen quiet minutes under the dome, two minutes from Amalienborg, straight after the guard change. On Day 2 you see it from the square instead, frontlit at mid-morning, which is the better photograph of the two.
Begun1749
Consecrated1894 — 145 years later
Dome span31 m, the largest in Scandinavia
CostFree; tower climb ticketed in summer

The church was meant to crown Frederik V’s new district and was begun in 1749 in expensive Norwegian marble. The money ran out. Building stopped in 1770 when the king died, and the half-finished shell stood as a ruin in the middle of the planned quarter for most of a century — an embarrassment familiar enough that Copenhageners simply called it the marble church, which is still its name.

It was finally completed on private money in the 1870s and 80s, in cheaper limestone rather than marble, and consecrated in 1894. The dome, carried on twelve columns, spans 31 metres and is the largest in Scandinavia.

What to look for

Go in and look straight up: the interior of the dome is painted with the twelve apostles, and the acoustics under it are startling. From outside, the building is the fixed point of Eigtved’s plan — the whole Frederiksstaden district is arranged so that this dome closes the view from Amalienborg’s courtyard.

In summer there are guided climbs up into the dome itself, which come out on an external balcony with a view over the palace and the harbour. That is a separate ticket and a fixed departure time, and it is not in your plan.
