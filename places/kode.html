KODE Art Museums

Four buildings on a city lake holding 50,000 works — and one collection inside them worth going for.

Day 11 · Tue Aug 18 · Rain-day alternativeSecond choice on a washout day, after Schøtstuene. Do not try to see all four buildings — go to the Rasmus Meyer Collection and give it the time.
BuildingsFour, around Lille Lungegårdsvannet
Collection~50,000 works
Go forThe Rasmus Meyer Collection — Munch, J.C. Dahl, Harriet Backer
Also part of KODETroldhaugen, Grieg’s villa, out of town

KODE is one of the largest art institutions in the Nordic countries, spread across four buildings on the edge of the little city-centre lake. That scale is a trap for a visitor with one afternoon: the sensible approach is to pick a collection rather than a museum.

The one to pick is the Rasmus Meyer Collection. Meyer was a Bergen businessman who bought Norwegian art intensively in the early 20th century and left it to the city in 1916. It covers what is usually called the golden age of Norwegian painting — Edvard Munch in real depth, including major works from the 1890s, plus J.C. Dahl, whose fjord landscapes effectively invented the visual idea of Norway, and Harriet Backer, whose interiors are quietly among the best paintings in the country.

The other buildings hold decorative arts and silver, international modernism, and changing contemporary exhibitions. Grieg’s villa at Troldhaugen is also administratively part of KODE.
