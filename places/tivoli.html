Tivoli Gardens

An 1843 pleasure garden in the middle of the capital, and the direct ancestor of every theme park since.

Georg Carstensen persuaded Christian VIII to grant him land outside the western gate for a pleasure garden, reportedly with the argument that “when the people are amusing themselves, they do not think about politics.” Tivoli opened in August 1843 and has been running ever since, which makes it the second-oldest operating amusement park in the world.

It is not a theme park in the modern sense and does not try to be. It is a garden first: flowerbeds, a lake left over from the old city moat, a Chinese-style pantomime theatre from 1874, a concert hall, restaurants, and rides threaded between the trees rather than laid out in zones. Walt Disney visited in 1951 and took notes; the influence on Disneyland is direct and acknowledged.

What to look for after dark

The lighting is the whole point and it is why an evening visit beats an afternoon one. Thousands of bulbs, lanterns over the lake, and the Nimb building’s Moorish facade lit up. The Rutschebanen, built in 1914, is one of the world’s oldest wooden coasters still running and still carries a brakeman in the train to control its speed on the descents.

Practical

Entry and rides are separate. If nobody wants to ride, the wristband is wasted money and the garden alone is worth the evening. The food hall inside handles dinner, and kitchens run to 23:00. With the Copenhagen Card, re-entry on another day is free, which is why Day 3’s evening lists it as an option.
