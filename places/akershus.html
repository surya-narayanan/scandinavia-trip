Akershus Fortress

Akershus festning

A medieval castle on the harbour that has never been taken by an enemy in a siege.

Day 6 · Thu Aug 13 · 14:25–15:25Free ramparts and a harbour panorama five minutes from your apartment — an easy hour that costs nothing, with the Opera House roof immediately afterwards.
Begunc. 1290s, by Håkon V
Sieges survivedRepeatedly besieged, never taken by assault
SurrenderedWithout a fight, to Germany in 1940
CostGrounds free; castle interior and museums ticketed

Håkon V began the castle at the end of the 13th century to guard the approach to Oslo, and it worked: Akershus was besieged many times, by Swedes and by Danes, and was never taken by force. Christian IV rebuilt it as a Renaissance royal residence in the early 1600s, which is the shape you see now — a castle inside a bastioned fortress inside a still-active military headquarters.

Its darker history is 20th-century. The fortress was handed over without resistance in April 1940 and used by the German occupation; executions were carried out here, and after the war Norwegian collaborators including Vidkun Quisling were executed on the same ground. The Norwegian Resistance Museum stands inside the walls.

What to look for

Walk the ramparts on the harbour side for the view across Oslofjord and back at the Opera House and Barcode towers — the contrast between the medieval stone and the glass city is the photograph. The royal mausoleum and the banqueting halls are inside the castle proper, which is a separate ticket and not in your plan. The grounds stay open late.
