Fantoft Stave Church

Fantoft stavkirke

A stave church built around 1150, moved here in 1883, burned by arsonists in 1992, and rebuilt to match.

Not currently in the planLeft out, and the honest reason is the combination of the round trip and what you would be looking at. It is 40 minutes of travel for a building that is, since 1992, a reconstruction rather than the medieval original — and it does not open until about 10:30, so it cannot be squeezed into the Aug 20 airport morning either.

Getting thereBybanen line 1 to Fantoft, ~20 min each way; ~NOK 70; opens ~10:30

The church was built at Fortun in Sogn around 1150 and stood there for over 700 years. In 1883, when it was due for demolition, the Bergen consul Fredrik Gade bought it and had it moved here to his estate at Fantoft — a common enough rescue at the time, and the reason several stave churches survive at all.

On 6 June 1992 it was deliberately burned down. The arson was one of a series of church fires in Norway connected to the early black metal scene, and it became one of the defining incidents of that period; Varg Vikernes was widely associated with it, though convicted for other fires.

It was rebuilt over the following five years as a faithful copy, using traditional methods, and finished in 1997. What stands now is accurate and atmospheric — dark tarred staves, dragon heads on the gables, a hillside of pines — but it is thirty years old, and that is worth knowing before you spend a morning on it.
