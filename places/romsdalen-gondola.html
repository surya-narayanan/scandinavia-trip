Åndalsnes sits at the head of Romsdalsfjord where the Rauma river comes in, ringed by some of the most vertical terrain in Norway. Until 2021 getting above the town meant a steep hike up Nesaksla; the gondola now does it in about five minutes, and it has transformed what a stopover here can be.

From the deck at 708 metres the fjord runs one way and the Romsdalen valley the other. Directly across the valley is Trollveggen, the Troll Wall — Europe’s tallest vertical rock face, about 1,100 metres of overhanging granite, and a place with a long history of fatal BASE jumps, now banned. The Rauma railway you arrived on runs along the valley floor beneath it.

At the top

There is a restaurant, Eggen, cantilevered out over the drop, and a flat viewing deck — no scrambling required to get the view. Waffles are the standard order. Marked trails continue along the ridge for anyone who wants more, and it is possible to walk down, but with a train and a bus to catch this is not the day for it.

If cloud is sitting on the summit the ride is pointless; the Norsk Tindesenter mountaineering museum at the gondola base is the indoor alternative and has a climbing wall.
