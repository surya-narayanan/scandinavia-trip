Strandvägen

The boulevard built to impress the world in 1897 — and still the most expensive address in Sweden.

Day 15 · Sat Aug 22 · 17:00–18:30The walk home from the museum, and it ends near your neighbourhood. Golden light at this hour, and dinner en route rather than a separate stop.
Laid out1860s–1890s, completed for the 1897 Stockholm exhibition
LengthAbout 1 km along the Östermalm waterfront
Known forOrnate apartment palaces, moored classic boats, a triple avenue of limes
CostFree

Until the mid-19th century this was a working shoreline of jetties, timber yards and sheds. The city decided to replace it with a boulevard on the Parisian model, and the work was finished in time for the great Stockholm exhibition of 1897 — the point being to give visitors arriving by sea something spectacular to look at.

It worked. The buildings along it are the high point of Swedish late-19th-century apartment architecture: turrets, bays, carved stone, elaborate stairwells, built for the wealthiest families in the country and still among the most valuable property in Sweden. The street is laid out on three levels — the roadway, a raised lime avenue, and the quay below.

Along the quay are moored wooden boats, including some of the classic steamers that still run summer services out into the archipelago. It ends at Djurgårdsbron, the bridge over to Djurgården, which is where you will cross on Day 17 for the Vasa.
