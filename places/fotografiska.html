Fotografiska

A photography museum in a 1906 customs house, open till 23:00, with a top-floor restaurant looking back at the old city.

Day 17 · Mon Aug 24 · 18:15–20:30The last-night finale, and the reason it works at this hour is the 23:00 closing — no other museum in the city is available after dinner. Book the restaurant table; it is on your checklist and it is a proper destination rather than a museum café.

Fotografiska took over a heavy brick customs house of 1906 on the Södermalm waterfront and turned it into a photography venue with no permanent collection at all — everything is temporary exhibitions, four or five major shows a year, and the roster has run from Nan Goldin and Sebastião Salgado to Annie Leibovitz and David LaChapelle. Whatever is on when you arrive is what you get, so check the programme rather than expecting particular work.

The commercial model is unusual and it is why the late hours exist: it is a private venture rather than a state museum, with a bar and a restaurant doing serious business, and the building stays open until 23:00.

The restaurant

On the top floor, with a glass wall looking north across the water at Gamla Stan and the city — one of the better dining views in Stockholm. It is strongly vegetarian-oriented, largely plant-based and seasonal, and it is consistently well reviewed rather than being a museum restaurant coasting on the view. Book ahead for a last night.
