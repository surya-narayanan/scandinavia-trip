Freetown Christiania

Fristaden Christiania

An abandoned naval barracks squatted in 1971, still governed by consensus meeting fifty years later.

Day 4 · Tue Aug 11 · 16:00–16:45Three minutes’ walk from Our Saviour’s, and 45 minutes is the right length — the main drag and the lakeside paths, not a deep exploration. The photography rule is not a suggestion: cameras out in the wrong part of it will get you challenged, and the request is worth respecting on its own terms.

The Bådsmandsstræde barracks on the Christianshavn ramparts were abandoned by the Danish military in 1971. Squatters moved in that September — partly for housing, partly as a deliberate experiment — declared the area a free town, and wrote a constitution whose stated aim was a self-governing society where each individual is responsible to the whole community. Decisions are still taken by consensus in a common meeting rather than by vote.

Its legal status has been contested for the entire fifty-plus years. Residents bought the land collectively from the state through a foundation in 2011, which settled the question of tenure without settling much else. Cannabis was sold openly on Pusher Street for decades in an uneasy standoff with police; in 2024 the residents themselves tore up the street’s paving to end the trade after years of gang violence, which is the most significant thing to have happened here in a generation.

What to look for

Self-built architecture, much of it genuinely inventive — houses assembled from salvage on the rampart banks and around the lake, a few of them now studied by architects. Murals, workshops, the Grey Hall concert venue in a former riding house, and cargo-bike builders: the Christiania bike, with its box over the front wheel, was invented here and is now everywhere in Copenhagen.

The lakeside path around the old ramparts is the quiet half and the better walk. Cars are banned throughout.
