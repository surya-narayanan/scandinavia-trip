Kronborg Castle

Kronborg Slot

Hamlet’s Elsinore: a fortress built to tax every ship entering the Baltic, at the narrowest point of the Øresund.

Kronborg exists because of a toll. From 1429 Danish kings charged every ship passing through the Øresund, and this is the narrowest point — four kilometres across to Sweden. The guns here and at Helsingborg opposite could close the strait, and the Sound Dues they enforced were for centuries the single largest source of Danish crown revenue. Frederik II rebuilt the medieval fortress into a Renaissance palace between 1574 and 1585 to advertise where that money had gone.

Shakespeare never came, but English actors touring Denmark in the 1580s did, and the castle was famous enough by 1600 to serve as Elsinore in Hamlet. The association has been commercially useful ever since; the courtyard hosts a Shakespeare festival most summers.

Inside

The casemates in the base are the best part and the coldest — dark vaulted passages built to shelter and supply a garrison under siege, where the statue of Holger the Dane sits asleep, the folk-hero who will wake when Denmark is in danger. Above are the royal apartments, the chapel with its original 16th-century carved pews, and the ballroom, at 62 metres one of the largest halls in northern Europe when it was built.

The ramparts

The outer works are a complete 17th-century bastioned fortress, and the cannon terrace faces straight out at Sweden across the shipping lane. In clear light this is the best view of the day. It is also exposed — expect wind in the 20s of km/h with gusts far higher, and hold onto hats and lens caps.
