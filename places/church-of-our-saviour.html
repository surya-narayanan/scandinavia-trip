Church of Our Saviour

Vor Frelsers Kirke

A baroque church in Christianshavn with an external corkscrew staircase winding up the outside of its spire.

Day 4 · Tue Aug 11 · 15:10–16:00This is the one item where your group splits by ability. The climb is 400 steps and the last 150 are outside, on a staircase that narrows as it spirals — climbers only, about 35 minutes round trip. The baroque interior at ground level is free and genuinely worth the visit on its own. Book the timed ticket 1–2 days ahead; the card does not cover it.

The church itself is a solid Dutch-influenced baroque box, consecrated in 1695 to serve the new merchant and naval district of Christianshavn. What everyone comes for was added fifty years later: Lauritz de Thurah’s spire, finished in 1752, with a helical staircase that leaves the interior and winds up the outside of the tower to a gilded globe and figure of Christ at the top.

It turns anticlockwise. A persistent Copenhagen story says the architect got the direction wrong, realised it on the day of the unveiling and threw himself off — de Thurah in fact died in his bed seven years later, and the twist is deliberate.

The climb, honestly

Four hundred steps. The interior portion is ordinary tower stairs through the bell chamber. The final 150 are the point and the problem: an open external staircase, exposed to the wind, with the treads getting narrower and the handrail getting lower the higher you go. There is nothing dangerous about it and there is no space to change your mind halfway.

Inside, at ground level

Free, flat, and often skipped by people fixated on the spire. The 1698 organ is carried on two carved elephants, there is a baroque altarpiece in marble and alabaster, and the royal box is at gallery level. The square outside is a good place to wait.
