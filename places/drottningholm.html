Drottningholm Palace

Drottningholms slott

The private residence of the Swedish royal family, and the best-preserved royal palace of its period in northern Europe.

Day 16 · Sun Aug 23 · 10:30–12:00The arrival by water is genuinely part of the visit — book the boat and palace as a combination. Ninety minutes inside, then lunch in the gardens.
BuiltFrom 1662, by Nicodemus Tessin the Elder and Younger

Queen Hedvig Eleonora commissioned Drottningholm in 1662, on an island in Lake Mälaren, and the Tessins — father and son, the leading architects of Swedish baroque — built it as a deliberate answer to Versailles at a scale Sweden could afford. It has been added to in every century since without ever being wrecked, which is why UNESCO calls it the best-preserved royal palace of its era in northern Europe.

It is also still a home. The royal family moved here permanently in 1981 and lives in the southern wing; the rest is open to the public. That gives the visit a slightly different quality from a purely museum palace.

What to see

The staircase, with trompe-l’œil painting and sculpture designed to disorient; Hedvig Eleonora’s state bedchamber, the most lavish baroque interior in Sweden and used for ceremonial rather than sleeping; the library of Gustav III; and the Karl X Gustav gallery. The formal baroque garden to the west has parterres and bronze sculptures looted from Prague and Denmark in the 17th century — the spoils are the point, and they were displayed as such.

Beyond the formal garden the grounds turn into an English landscape park, which is free and good for the packed lunch.
