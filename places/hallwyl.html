The Hallwyl Museum

Hallwylska museet

A 1890s private palace left exactly as its owner catalogued it, on the condition that nothing ever be moved.

Wilhelmina von Hallwyl was the daughter of a timber millionaire and married into a Swiss noble family. She built this house on Hamngatan in the 1890s — with electricity, central heating, telephones and bathrooms, all unusual at the time — and then spent decades filling it with collections: paintings, arms and armour, porcelain, silver, textiles, coins, her own household inventory down to the kitchen equipment.

What makes it extraordinary is what she did next. She had the entire contents catalogued in exhaustive detail across 78 volumes, and in 1920 donated the whole house and everything in it to the Swedish state on the explicit condition that nothing be rearranged. She and her husband went on living there until their deaths. It opened as a museum in 1938, and it is not a reconstruction of a period interior — it is the interior, unmoved.

What to look for

The billiard room, the smoking room and the grand salon, all fully furnished; the bathroom, which was startling for 1898; and the servants’ areas and kitchen, preserved with the same rigour as the state rooms, which is rarer than it should be. Entry is free.
