Roskilde Cathedral

Roskilde Domkirke

The burial church of the Danish monarchy — forty kings and queens in one building, across eight centuries of changing taste.

Absalon, the same bishop who founded Copenhagen, began this church in the 1170s. It was one of the first Gothic buildings in brick in northern Europe, and it set the pattern for church building across the Baltic. But its real significance is dynastic: from the 15th century almost every Danish monarch has been buried here, and the practice continues.

That makes it a peculiar and rewarding building to walk through, because each ruler was buried in the fashion of their own century. You move from medieval brick chapels to Renaissance marble to a stark modernist burial chapel for Frederik IX outside the east end, and the sequence is a legible history of Danish taste. The current queen’s sarcophagus, designed in her lifetime, is a deliberate break with all of it.

What to look for

The Chapel of the Magi, with Christian III’s tomb and the pillar where visiting royalty had their heights marked. The astronomical clock above the west end, where a figure of St George strikes a dragon on the hour and the dragon shrieks. The carved 15th-century choir stalls. And Margrete I’s alabaster sarcophagus behind the altar — she ruled Denmark, Norway and Sweden as one realm from 1397.
