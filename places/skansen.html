Skansen

The world’s first open-air museum, opened 1891 — a country’s worth of relocated buildings, plus its native animals.

Day 17 · Mon Aug 24 · 16:10–17:40 — optionalExplicitly the designated cut on this day, and skipping it is not a loss — Vasa is the summit of the afternoon and Fotografiska closes the evening. Take it only if energy is genuinely high at 16:00.
Founded1891, by Artur Hazelius — the first open-air museum anywhere
WhereOn the heights of Djurgården, next to the Vasa
Contains~150 relocated historic buildings, plus a zoo of Nordic species
Cost~SEK 260
Open till18:00

Artur Hazelius watched Swedish rural culture disappearing under industrialisation and in 1891 did something nobody had done before: he bought a hilltop on Djurgården and began moving whole buildings onto it from across the country, to be shown in use rather than as objects. Every open-air museum in the world descends from this one — including Norsk Folkemuseum in Oslo and Gamle Bergen, both of which you have encountered on this trip.

There are around 150 buildings: farmsteads, a manor house, a glassworks, a bakery, a print shop, a whole town quarter of 19th-century urban trades, with staff working them in period dress. And a zoo of Nordic animals — bears, wolves, lynx, wolverines, moose, seals — which is the part that makes it a genuinely different proposition from the Oslo equivalent.

It is large, it is hilly, and 90 minutes is thin for it. That is part of why it is the cut.
