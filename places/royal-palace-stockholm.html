The Royal Palace

Kungliga slottet

One of the largest palaces in Europe still in official use — 600-odd rooms built on the burnt-out shell of a medieval castle.

The medieval castle of Tre Kronor stood here for four centuries and burned down in 1697. Nicodemus Tessin the Younger — the same architect as Drottningholm — was already drawing plans to modernise it and simply built his baroque palace around the surviving north wall. Work ran on until 1760, slowed by Sweden’s wars, and the result is about six hundred rooms wrapped around a courtyard.

It is not the royal residence — the family lives at Drottningholm — but it remains the official palace, used for state occasions, and the guard is mounted daily.

What to do with one combined ticket

The State Apartments are the main sequence: Karl XI’s gallery, done in the manner of Versailles’ Hall of Mirrors, the state bedchamber, the ballroom, and the Bernadotte apartments. The Treasury in the cellar vaults holds the Swedish regalia — the crown, orb, sceptre and key of Erik XIV from the 1560s, and Gustav Vasa’s sword of state. Unlike the Danish regalia these are no longer worn: they are placed on cushions beside the monarch at ceremonies.

Also included, and skippable on a tight morning: the Royal Armoury in the cellars, which is genuinely strange and good — royal costumes including the clothes Gustav III was shot in at the masked ball, and state coaches; and the Tre Kronor museum, the excavated cellars of the medieval castle that burned.
