Oslo Opera House

Operahuset

A building designed to be walked on: a white marble slope rising straight out of the fjord.

Day 6 · Thu Aug 13 · 15:35–16:30The whole point here is the roof, and the ramp up is gentle enough for everyone — no stairs required. Free, always open, and five minutes from Akershus.
Opened2008
ArchitectsSnøhetta
PrizeMies van der Rohe Award, 2009
CostFree to walk the roof, always open

Snøhetta won the competition with an idea rather than a facade: make the roof a public square and let the building rise out of the water as a slope people can climb. It opened in 2008, clad in white Carrara marble and granite, and the gesture worked — the roof is used constantly, by people with no interest in opera, as a place to sit and look at the fjord.

The interior is a deliberate contrast: a wave-shaped wall of oak strips curving through the foyer, wrapping the auditorium like the hull of a boat. You can walk into the foyer for free even without a ticket, and it is worth doing for that wall alone.

What to look for

From the top of the slope you get Oslofjord, the Barcode office towers, Akershus across the water and the Munch museum next door. In the water just off the building floats She Lies, a steel-and-glass sculpture by Monica Bonvicini modelled on a Caspar David Friedrich painting of an ice wreck; it rotates with the tide.

The marble is slippery when wet and the slope is unforgiving about footwear.
