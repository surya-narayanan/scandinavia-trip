Gamle Bergen

Old Bergen Museum

About fifty wooden houses moved to Sandviken and laid out as streets and yards — the city as it was when it was the largest wooden city in Europe.

Not currently in the planGenuinely good and warmly recommended by locals, but it loses to the fortress interiors on a single Bergen day: it is 4 km out plus a bus, and the round trip is about two hours you do not have. This is the first thing to add if you ever get a second day in Bergen.
WhereSandviken, ~4 km north of the centre
Getting thereBus 3, 4, 12, 16E or 19 from Bryggen
SeasonRoughly mid-May to end of August
Time needed~2 hrs round trip from the centre

In the 19th century Bergen was the largest wooden city in Europe, and most of it is gone — lost to fire, to demolition, and to the concrete of the 20th century. Gamle Bergen collects what survived: around fifty timber houses from the 1700s, 1800s and early 1900s, moved to a park in Sandviken and arranged as actual streets, alleys and back yards rather than as isolated exhibits.

The interiors are furnished by period and trade — a photographer’s studio, a dentist, a baker, a sailmaker, middle-class parlours and workers’ single rooms — and in season are staffed by guides in costume. Walking a street of it gives you something the surviving fragments in the city centre cannot: the sense of a whole town built of painted wood.

The grounds and the setting on the fjord are free to walk; the house interiors are seen on guided tours.
