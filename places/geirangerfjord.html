Geirangerfjord

A 15-kilometre arm of water between cliffs 1,400 metres high, with abandoned farms still clinging to the ledges.

Geirangerfjord is a textbook glacial trough: a valley gouged below sea level by ice, then flooded when the ice left. The walls rise to about 1,400 metres and the water goes down to 260, so the rock face effectively continues far beneath your keel. At its narrowest the fjord is only 250 metres across, which is what produces the sense of sailing down a corridor.

The waterfalls

De syv søstrene, the Seven Sisters, drop about 250 metres in seven separate streams down the north wall. Facing them across the water is Friaren, the Suitor, whose shape is said to resemble a bottle — the local story being that he courted all seven sisters, was refused by each, and took to drink. Further along, Brudesløret, the Bridal Veil, falls in a thin sheet that the boat passes close enough to feel in a westerly.

The cliff farms

Look up at the ledges for Skageflå, Knivsflå and Blomberg — farms worked until the early 20th century on shelves hundreds of metres above the water, reached only by rope ladders that could be pulled up. Children were reportedly tethered so they could not fall. They were abandoned when the tax collector could finally reach them, which is the joke locals still tell, and several are now maintained as heritage sites.
