Hardangerfjord

Norway’s second-longest fjord, and its orchard country — the one fjord where the shores are farmed rather than empty.

Day 12 · Wed Aug 19 · 10:00–10:20 viewpoint, and most of the day’s drivingThe fjord is the through-line of the whole Hardanger day rather than a single stop — you get a dedicated viewpoint at 10:00 and then drive its shores for hours, crossing the Hardanger Bridge in the afternoon.
Length179 km — second only to Sognefjord
DepthUp to 800 m
Known forApples, pears, plums and cider; blossom in May
Crossed byThe Hardanger Bridge, 1,380 m, opened 2013

Hardangerfjord runs 179 kilometres inland from the Atlantic, which makes it the second-longest in Norway and the fourth-longest in the world, and it reaches depths of around 800 metres. What distinguishes it from Geirangerfjord is not scale but use: the Hardanger shores are gentle enough, and the microclimate mild enough, to farm.

This is Norway’s fruit district. The slopes are terraced with apple, pear, plum and cherry orchards, and it has a serious cider industry with protected-origin status. In May the blossom draws visitors the way the autumn colour does elsewhere; in August you are seeing it in full green with fruit on the trees.

The Hardanger Bridge, which your tour crosses in the afternoon, opened in 2013 with a 1,380-metre main span — among the longest suspension bridges in the world — and replaced the ferry that used to cut the region in two. Above the fjord to the east rises the Hardangervidda, the largest mountain plateau in northern Europe, and the Folgefonna glacier sits on the peninsula to the south.
