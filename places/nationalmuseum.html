Nationalmuseum

Sweden’s national gallery, on the water opposite the palace — Rembrandt, the Scandinavian rooms, and a design floor running to the present.

The Nationalmuseum holds the Swedish state art collection — around 700,000 objects, of which perhaps five thousand are on show — in a Renaissance-revival building of 1866 on Blasieholmen, looking across the water at the Royal Palace. It closed for five years and reopened in 2018 with the interiors substantially restored and the hang completely rethought.

What is here

The old-master holdings are strong in Dutch and Flemish painting — several Rembrandts, including The Conspiracy of the Batavians, plus Rubens and a good group of genre painters. There is a fine 18th-century French collection, largely assembled by Count Carl Gustaf Tessin, and Chardin’s work is unusually well represented.

But the reason to come is the Swedish and Nordic rooms: Carl Larsson’s huge murals on the staircase, Anders Zorn, Bruno Liljefors, and the Nordic light painters of the 1880s and 90s. And above them a design floor that runs from 18th-century furniture through to 20th-century Swedish modernism and contemporary work — which in Stockholm is not a footnote.

If you only have the closing hour: staircase, Scandinavian painting, design floor. Skip the old masters and do not attempt the whole building.
