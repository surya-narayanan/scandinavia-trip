Christiansborg is the only building in the world housing all three branches of a country’s government: the Folketing, the Supreme Court and the prime minister’s office. It stands on Slotsholmen, the small island where Bishop Absalon built the castle that started Copenhagen in 1167 — and the excavated ruins of that castle are still in the basement.

Two previous palaces on the site burned, in 1794 and 1884. The 1794 fire is the reason the royal family moved to Amalienborg and stayed. The present building went up between 1907 and 1928 in a heavy neo-baroque, and its granite facade was funded partly by a public subscription.

The tower

At 106 m this is the tallest tower in Copenhagen, and since 2014 it has been free to the public — an elevator, no ticket, no booking. From the top you can see the whole spire-line of the old city laid out at once: Our Saviour’s corkscrew, the Marble Church dome, the Round Tower, the Stock Exchange, the harbour and across to Sweden on a clear day. It is the single best orientation stop in Copenhagen and most visitors miss it.

Note the closure: Mondays. There is a restaurant at the top if you want to sit.
