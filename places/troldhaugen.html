Troldhaugen

Edvard Grieg’s villa above a lake, with the hut where he actually composed still standing at the water’s edge.

Not currently in the planNot in the plan purely on distance — 45 minutes each way by public transport is a half-day, and you have one Bergen day with a fjord cruise in the morning. Worth knowing it exists if a future trip gives you more time.
Built1885
Museum since1928
IncludesThe villa, the composing hut, the Griegs’ grave, and Troldsalen, a 200-seat concert hall
Getting there~20 min by car, ~45 min by public transport
Part ofKODE

Grieg built Troldhaugen — troll hill — in 1885 and lived here for the last 22 summers of his life. He called it his “best opus so far.” The villa is preserved with his furniture and his Steinway, which is still played at recitals.

The building that matters most is the smallest: a one-room wooden hut on the slope down to Lake Nordås, where he shut himself in to work. Almost everything he wrote after 1885 was written in it. He and his wife Nina, a singer and his cousin, are buried in a rock tomb in the cliff below the house.

A modern concert hall, Troldsalen, seats 200 with a glass wall looking out at the composing hut — lunchtime recitals run through the summer, which is the best way to see the place.
