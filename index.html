11:50–12:35
★ AMALIENBORG SQUARE + CHANGING OF THE GUARD — the set-piece you have not seen
Four identical rococo palaces around an octagonal courtyard, built in the 1750s as noblemen's mansions and only taken over by the royals after Christiansborg burned in 1794. Saly's equestrian Frederik V stands at the centre — it took three decades and reportedly cost more than the buildings around it. The ceremony arrives at 12:00 and runs to about 12:30, and where you stand matters: since Frederik X's accession the King's Guard forms up in front of Frederik VIII's Palace — the northeast one, and the only one of the four with a clock on its facade. Face the clock. At noon in August that facade takes the sun, so it is also the right side for photographs.
⚠ THE BAND IS NOT GUARANTEED. Music only when the monarch is in residence — Kongevagt rather than Palævagt — and mid-August is prime royal-summer season. The royal standard flying over a palace means the King is in it; check the flagpoles as you walk in so nobody is waiting on a brass band that was never coming. Positioning by 11:50 is enough — the courtyard is large.
Square + ceremony FREE

Severe. Aug 9 is a Sunday and nearly everything independent is shut; only the department stores save you.
Milder — Stockholm keeps more Sunday trading, and Aug 23 is the Sunday you have a free late afternoon anyway.

⚠ THE ONE REAL TRAP — SUNDAY AUG 16 IN ÅLESUND. Norwegian law closes most shops on Sundays; only premises under 100 m² may open, and only ~6 hours (tourist towns get partial exemptions, so expect a lottery).
