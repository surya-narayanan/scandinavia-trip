ON THE BRYGGEN FRONTAGE — WHAT TO PICK OUT. The wharf is not one building but a row of separate tenements, each with its own name and its own passage running back from the quay. Walking north-east from the Fish Market toward Bergenhus, look for:
• Jacobsfjorden — a gilded deer head mounted on the front facade, which is why it is also called Hjortegården, the Deer Courtyard. The most findable single detail on the whole wharf.
• Bellgården — around 300 years old, and it forms a double courtyard together with Jacobsfjorden: go into one passage and out through the other.
• Enhjørningsgården — the Unicorn Courtyard.
• Bredsgården — one of the best-preserved passages, and the 18th-century dining rooms of Bryggen Tracteursted are in it; worth stepping into even if you are not eating.
• Svensgården, Bugården, Holmedalsgården, Engelgården — the rest of the surviving rows, and the ones with the most visible lean.
• Finnegården, Bryggen 39 — the Hanseatic Museum building. Under restoration until about 2027, so note the facade and do not queue at the door.
• Øvregaten — the back lane running parallel behind the whole wharf, and the street your Fjellveien descent comes down onto. Schøtstuene is at no. 50 and Mariakirken is at its north end.

PLAN B — split by weather, decided each morning

⚠ NOT THE PICK IF THE FORECAST HOLDS — with two clear days this plan solves a problem you no longer have. Keep it as the fallback if the forecast turns.

The Bergen-native one. You do not assign the days in advance. Each morning you look at the forecast and run whichever plan fits: the clear day is all views and outdoors, the wet day is all interiors. Bergen’s weather changes several times a day and the odds of two identical days are low, so this maximises the chance that Ulriken and Fløyen both get visibility — which is the single thing most worth protecting, because a 643 m viewpoint in cloud is nothing at all.

THE COST OF PLAN B: you cannot pre-book timed tickets, because you do not know which day is which. Fløibanen and Ulriken both sell on the day at the station, so that is fine; it is the Bymuseet sites and any restaurant booking that need to stay flexible. Also, whichever plan you run second is fixed by elimination, so a wet second day means the interiors day happens in the rain and the clear day is already banked — which is the right way round.
